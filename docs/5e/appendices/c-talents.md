# Appendix C — Talents (5e core)

All 167 talents in the 5e core book have a 4e counterpart (five were renamed). The main structural change is that 5e talents no longer have a maximum rank based on a Characteristic Bonus: most can be taken once, a few a fixed number of times, and specialisable talents once per specialisation. Every talent costs a flat 100 XP per take (4e: 100 XP × rank). 4e's per-talent **Tests** line is gone — 5e expresses those bonuses as *Advantage on X Tests* inside the description.

Columns: *4e max* is what Hammergen stores today (`maxRank`, or the characteristic bonus in `attribute` when `maxRank` is 0). *5e max* is read from the talent text. *Mechanics* lists effects Hammergen calculates, or could calculate, on the character sheet; empty means the effect is narrative/situational only.

Notes and oddities found while reading:

- The random talent table (p. 23) lists **Resistance (Poison)**, and the Apothecary, Warrior-type careers and Mark of Nurgle refer to **Resistance (…)**, but the talent itself is called **Resistant (Threat)** (p. 124).
- The Spellcaster Lord template (p. 354) lists **Instinctive Diction 2**, but the talent text has no ranks.
- **Etiquette**, **Hatred** and **Impassioned Zeal** take a specialisation but the text does not say they can be taken more than once; careers do grant e.g. *Etiquette (All)* and several different Etiquette specialisations.
- **Small** grants Size (Small), but the Size table (p. 360) lists only Tiny/Average/Large/Enormous/Monstrous and puts halflings in *Average*.
- Talents granted by creature traits or mutations (e.g. *Mark of Nurgle* lets you buy talents as if in career) are not modelled in Hammergen today.
